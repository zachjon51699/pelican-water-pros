WHEN REPLACEMENT MAKES SENSE

Signs You Need a New Water Heater

These are common reasons Baton Rouge homeowners start looking at replacement. None of them automatically means the tank has to come out today. They do mean it is time for a professional look.

  • Water heater leaking: Water at the base, a wet drain pan, or ceiling stains under an attic heater can mean the tank or a fitting has failed. A leak is a strong replacement signal, especially on an older tank.
  • No hot water: A silent tank with cold taps may have a failed element, thermostat, or a unit that has reached the end of its life. We sort that out before recommending a new heater.
  • Inconsistent water temperature: Hot one minute and lukewarm the next can come from a failing thermostat, sediment, or a tank that can no longer keep up with the household.
  • Rust or corrosion: Rusty water from hot taps, flaking at the tank, or a corroded anode often means the steel tank is aging out.
  • Aging water heater: Many residential tanks last about eight to twelve years depending on use and water conditions. Age alone is not a diagnosis, but it matters when another part fails.
  • Repeated breakdowns: Paying for the same repair twice in a short stretch is often more expensive than putting in a new unit.

EQUIPMENT AND ACCESS

Electric and Gas Water Heater Options

Most replacement jobs follow the fuel the house already uses. Electric water heater replacement is a service we currently offer for standard tank units. Many South Louisiana homes also have gas heaters. Gas work involves supply lines and venting, so we review those jobs individually instead of listing gas replacement as a standard menu item.

  • Electric tanks: Common in garages, closets, laundry rooms, and attics. We check the dedicated circuit, tank size, pan, and drain before swapping the unit.
  • Gas tanks: Require combustion air and a proper vent. Tell us on the quote form if the current heater is gas so we can confirm whether we can complete that replacement.
  • Attic installations: Attic tanks are common around Baton Rouge. Replacement usually takes extra labor because of access, hauling the old unit out, and setting the new tank in a tight space.

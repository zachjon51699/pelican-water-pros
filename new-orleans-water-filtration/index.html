Installation that fits the house

Why New Orleans Properties Need a Different Filtration Plan

Many New Orleans homes were never designed around a large mechanical room. Shotgun houses, camelbacks, and raised cottages may have the main water line in a tight side yard, under a raised floor, or in a small laundry closet. Condos and townhomes add another constraint: limited cabinet space and building rules about where equipment can sit. That is why we look at access, drain options, and service clearance before we recommend a whole house water filter.

When a point-of-entry system will not fit cleanly, a high-quality reverse osmosis tap at the kitchen sink is often the better first install. When there is room at the main line, whole-home carbon filtration can improve showers, laundry, and every faucet, which matters in a city where cooking, coffee, and entertaining are part of daily life. We explain those tradeoffs in plain language instead of selling the largest system that will physically squeeze in.

  • Compact kitchens: Under-sink reverse osmosis planned around disposals, pullouts, and existing shutoffs.
  • Raised and historic homes: Main-line access, outdoor equipment placement, and freeze/flood exposure considered before we mount tanks.
  • Whole-home where it fits: Point-of-entry filtration when plumbing and clearance support it.

Municipal water in New Orleans

City Water Treatment for Mississippi River Source Water

New Orleans municipal drinking water is drawn from the Mississippi River and treated by the Sewerage & Water Board of New Orleans. The utility uses chloramine as a disinfectant. That combination is why many households describe a lingering chemical taste or odor even when the water meets public-supply standards. Carbon filtration is often the starting point for taste and odor. Reverse osmosis is the usual next step when the kitchen tap is the priority.

Boil-water notices and main-break advisories happen in any older distribution system. Filtration is not a substitute for following official guidance during an advisory. After service is restored, homeowners sometimes still notice sediment or off tastes. That is a practical time to talk about city water treatment or a whole-home filter, not a reason to ignore the utility’s instructions while a notice is in effect.

Private wells are uncommon inside the city compared with the capital region. If you are on the Northshore, well water is more likely, and we will treat that as a different job than a Uptown or Mid-City municipal install.

Water Filtration Installation Questions from New Orleans Homeowners

Can you install a whole house water filter in a shotgun or raised cottage?

Sometimes. It depends on main-line access, a dry mounting location, and enough clearance to change filters later. If the only space is a cramped side yard or a flood-prone crawl area, we may recommend a point-of-use reverse osmosis system first and revisit whole-home equipment if a better location exists.

Will carbon filtration help with chloramine taste?

Catalytic or chloramine-rated carbon is commonly used for that complaint on municipal water. Results still depend on contact time, flow rate, and whether the carbon is maintained. We will not promise that every taste issue disappears, and we will say when reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap is the more realistic drinking-water upgrade.

Do I need a water softener in New Orleans?

Not automatically. Softeners address hardness minerals that cause scale and spotting. If your main issue is taste or odor from treated river water, carbon and reverse osmosis are usually the first conversation. We recommend a softener when you are actually seeing mineral buildup, not because every city home needs one.

Can filtration replace a boil-water notice?

No. During a boil-water advisory, follow the Sewerage & Water Board and local public-health instructions. After the notice is lifted, we can help with lingering sediment or taste concerns if you still want treatment at the house.

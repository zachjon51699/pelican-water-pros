Heating Elements, Electrical Requirements, and Common Tank Sizes

  • Heating elements: Upper and lower elements heat the stored water. A failed element can look like a dead heater. On a leaking or badly corroded tank, replacing the element is often a delay rather than a fix.
  • Electrical requirements: Most electric tanks need a dedicated 240-volt circuit and a disconnect that matches the nameplate. We do not treat a random outlet as acceptable power for a water heater.
  • Common tank sizes: 40- and 50-gallon tanks are typical in family homes. Smaller apartments and larger households sit on either side of that range. Matching the old gallon number is not always the right answer.

FREQUENTLY ASKED

Electric Water Heater FAQs

What tank sizes are common for electric water heaters?

Many homes use 40- or 50-gallon electric tanks. Larger households sometimes need more capacity. The right size depends on how many people use hot water at once, not only on what is already in the closet.

Do electric replacements need a new circuit?

Some replacements can use the existing dedicated circuit if it is in good condition and matches the new unit. Others need electrical updates. We will not guess that from a photo alone.

Can a failed heating element be the whole problem?

Sometimes an element or thermostat is the issue. If the tank is leaking, corroded, or old enough that another failure is likely, replacement is usually the more practical path. We do not recommend DIY electrical work on the heater.

Is an electric water heater a good fit for a Baton Rouge home?

Electric tanks are common in the area and avoid gas venting. They still need correct amperage, a safe drain pan where required, and a location that can be serviced later.
